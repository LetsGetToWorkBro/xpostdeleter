Facebook

The honest version, which you will not get from the tools selling you this: your personal timeline cannot be bulk-deleted by any API, ours included. Here is what can be automated, and how to do the rest by hand without getting your account locked.

What is and is not possible

Nobody can honestly promise more than this without breaking Meta's terms on your behalf.

Personal timeline posts cannot be deleted through any API. Meta removed publish_actions in Graph API v3.0 back in 2018 and never replaced it. The user_posts permission reads and nothing more. Any tool claiming to bulk-delete your timeline automatically is either driving your logged-in browser session or calling private endpoints. Both break Facebook's terms, and both are a well-known way to get locked out. We will not ship that.
What this does instead:
  • Deletes posts and your own comments on Facebook Pages you administer, fully automated, through the official Graph API.
  • Reads your Download Your Information export offline and tells you how much is where, so you know which years are worth attacking.
  • Walks you through Manage Activity, Facebook's own bulk tool, fifty items at a time.

Deleting a personal timeline, the supported way

Facebook's own bulk tool. Tedious, and the only method that will not put your account at risk.

1. Open Manage Activity

On desktop: your profile → ⋯ → Activity log → Manage activity → Your posts. On mobile it hides under Menu → Settings & privacy → Activity log.

2. One year at a time

Use the Date filter and start at your oldest year. Short lists keep the page responsive, and you can always tell where you got to.

3. Public posts first

Add Audience → Public. Those are the ones strangers can find, so clearing them buys you most of the benefit for the least clicking.

4. Select and bin, fifty at a time

Facebook caps selection at 50 items per action. Tick the header checkbox to grab the batch, pick Bin to delete or Archive to hide, and go again.

5. Empty the bin

Binned items sit there for 30 days before Facebook removes them properly. That is your undo window — and it means nothing is really gone until you empty it or wait it out.

6. Comments and likes are a separate job

Switch the dropdown from Your posts to Comments, Likes and reactions or Tags. Each is its own list with its own fifty-at-a-time limit.

Extensions that click those buttons for you are automating your logged-in session, which Facebook's terms forbid and its automation detection is looking for. Your account, your call — but this tool will not be the one that does it.
